abstract: |
  **Background.** Antibiotic therapy before presentation (prior antibiotic exposure, PAE) is reported in 11% to 39% of patients with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), depending strongly on the exposure window applied, yet its association with outcome remains unclear. We investigated whether it is independently associated with mortality in ambulatory and hospitalised patients and how it relates to detectable pathogens.

  **Methods.** Post-hoc analysis of the CAPNETZ cohort (n=11,830). Primary outcomes were 30- and 180-day mortality, stratified by treatment setting with a formal interaction test. Covariates were selected using a directed acyclic graph. Confounding was addressed by multiple-imputation logistic regression, supported by propensity-score matching, inverse-probability weighting and Cox regression. Adjusted absolute risks were calculated from the adjusted models.

  **Results.** PAE was documented in 3,034 patients (25.6%) and marked a younger, less severely ill phenotype. The unadjusted association with lower mortality was substantially attenuated after adjustment and the adjusted estimate was consistent across methods (180-day OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.80-1.18). There was no evidence that the association differed by treatment setting (interaction p=0.15). Among hospitalised patients the adjusted 180-day OR was 1.05 (0.86-1.29). Adjusted 180-day mortality was 7.13% without and 6.98% with PAE (risk difference -0.15 percentage points, -1.25 to +0.96). Pathogen detection was less frequent following PAE (16.1% vs 19.4%; adjusted OR 0.84, 0.75-0.94), with fewer pneumococcal and more atypical detections among pathogen-positive cases (both q&lt;0.001).

  **Conclusions.** In ambulatory and hospitalised CAP, PAE mainly marks a milder case mix and changes how microbiology should be interpreted, rather than being independently associated with prognosis.
