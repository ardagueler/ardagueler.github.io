abstract: |
  AI systems can quantify and communicate the uncertainty associated with their predictions, yet empirical evidence on how such information shapes human decision-making remains mixed. To consolidate the emerging literature, we systematically review 27 empirical studies on human decision-making under AI uncertainty. Our corpus shows that uncertainty communication affects human-AI decision-making at four distinct levels: cognitive processing, psychological responses, behavioral responses, and downstream decision performance. Although AI uncertainty often changes users' processing, trust, confidence, or reliance, these intermediate responses do not consistently translate into improved joint decision accuracy. The inconsistency suggests that the effectiveness of uncertainty communication depends on how the uncertainty representation aligns with the decision process, task context and user needs. Building on that insight, we consolidate the conditions for successful uncertainty communication into four alignment propositions that specify when uncertainty communication is likely to support trustworthy and effective human–AI decision-making. These propositions synthesize conditions related to representation format, cognitive demands, behavioral use, and performance relevance. By organizing the literature around these alignment conditions, our review clarifies why prior findings diverge and provides a conceptual foundation for future empirical research. For managers, the review highlights that uncertainty communication is not automatically beneficial; it must be designed and implemented in ways that help users act on uncertainty rather than merely perceive it to provide trustworthy AI systems.
