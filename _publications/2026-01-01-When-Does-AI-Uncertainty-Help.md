abstract: |
  Artificial intelligence (AI) systems (including machine learning systems) increasingly support human decision-making in highly consequential domains such as medical diagnosis and financial investment, where AI models may even exceed domain experts in predictive performance. However, even highly accurate AI systems are not infallible and can make wrong predictions and recommendations. In order to make effective decisions, users must therefore understand when to trust an AI system's prediction and when to override its recommendation. Users act on individual predictions whose reliability varies from case to case. For instance, commercial facial recognition systems classified gender with high overall accuracy but exhibited substantially higher error rates for underrepresented subgroups, misclassifying darker-skinned women in up to one third of cases. Such disparities, which can arise from biased training or model misspecification, illustrate a broader challenge: an AI system's predictive reliability is not uniform but varies across inputs and contexts.
