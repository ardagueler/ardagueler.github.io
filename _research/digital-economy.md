Digital platforms have become central intermediaries in the digital economy, and technologies such as smartphones, artificial intelligence, and tokenization keep expanding how firms reach consumers and what they sell. My research in this area studies both sides: how **digital interventions on platforms** influence consumer behavior, and how **emerging digital products** reshape strategic interactions and competition.

**Platforms.** In a research collaboration with an industry partner, I use **large-scale randomized field experiments** and **natural experiments** to study digital interventions such as mobile push notifications, including their indirect effects, spillovers across firms on the same platform, and personalization. I combine econometric approaches to causal inference with **causal machine learning** to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects, and use off-policy evaluation to assess targeting and personalization policies.

**Products.** Using **game-theoretic modeling** and numerical simulations, I study how digital technologies and products, such as tokenized digital products or smart durable goods, create new strategic opportunities and economic trade-offs for firms and platforms.
